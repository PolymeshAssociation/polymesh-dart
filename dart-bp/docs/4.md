# Settlement

A settlement can have 1 or more **Leg**s as each Leg represents one asset transfer. Say Alice wants to send 10 units of asset id $\mathrm{at}$ to Bob, then such a settlement will have 1 leg with Alice as sender and Bob as receiver.
If Alice wants to swap asset 10 units of $\mathrm{at}$ with Bob for 20 units of $\mathrm{at}'$, then such a settlement will have 2 legs with Alice acting as sender in one leg and receiver in other and vice versa for Bob.

Settlements might be created by sender, receiver or a third party. To keep the amount, and identities of parties confidential, the settlement creator encrypts these details including asset-id so that the chain does not even learn which asset is being traded. However, the chain knows how many legs the settlement has. The encryption is done for all parties to that leg which include sender, receiver and all auditors and mediators for that asset.
Following describes the creation of a leg, its encryption, decryption and the proof that the leg is correctly generated.

There are some options a creator has: It can choose to reveal the asset-id to the verifier (chain) making the proofs if the use-case permits, much proofs more efficient. 
It can also decide if sender and receiver learn each other's public key. Finally, it can choose to include additional auditors and mediators than already associated with the asset 
but these auditors and mediators keys become public and not hidden like the auditors and mediators keys associated with the asset. 

Note: Following is the proof for 1 leg only. A settlement is a collection of legs and thus collection of following proofs except the curve tree membership proof is batched.


## Leg

A leg has:

1. $\mathrm{EK}_s$ - Encryption public key of sender
2. $\mathrm{EK}_r$ - Encryption public key of receiver
3. $v$ - Amount of units being transferred
4. $\mathrm{at}$ - Asset-id being transferred
5. $\mathrm{enc\_keys}$ - List of encryption keys $\mathrm{EK}_i$ stored with the asset (hidden from verifier unless asset-id is revealed). These keys belong to auditors and mediators who share encryption keys.
6. $\mathrm{med\_keys}$ - List of mediator affirmation public keys $\mathrm{MK}_j$ (hidden from verifier unless asset-id is revealed). Each mediator also knows one of the $\mathrm{enc\_keys}$. The mediator affirmation key ciphertext is created for every $\mathrm{EK}_i$ (unless asset-id is revealed).
7. $\mathrm{public\_enc\_keys}$ - List of encryption keys provided by leg creator (always revealed to verifier). These act as additional auditors beyond those associated with the asset.

### Encryption
The settlement creator wants to ensure that the same leg encryption is given to all parties and thus uses twisted Elgamal.

Note that an encryption key can be shared between an auditor and a mediator (see system model in 1.md). The issuer distributes the corresponding encryption secret key using key-distribution-proof to all such parties before they start auditing/mediating. A mediator's affirmation key ciphertext $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ is encrypted so that any holder of any of the asset's encryption keys $\mathrm{EK}_i$ can recover it.

**Configuration flags.** The leg's `party_visibility` controls which of the two parties may learn the other's identity. It has four modes — `NoVisibility`, `FullVisibility`, `OnlySenderSeesReceiver`, `OnlyReceiverSeesSender` — decomposed into two independent booleans `sender_sees_receiver` and `receiver_sees_sender`:

- When `sender_sees_receiver`, the sender's ephemeral key carries an extra $\mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_2$ component (so the sender can decrypt the receiver's $\mathrm{EK}_r$ from $\mathrm{CT}_r$). When `receiver_sees_sender`, the receiver's carries an extra $\mathrm{EK}_r \cdot r_1$ component (so the receiver can decrypt $\mathrm{EK}_s$ from $\mathrm{CT}_s$). The two directions are independent; when neither holds the parties cannot learn each other's identity.
- Two derived booleans control which cross-ratios appear as witnesses in the Bulletproof commitment (see "Leg creation proof" below):
  - `commit_r_2_r_1_inv` = `sender_sees_receiver` OR (the asset has any encryption keys, i.e. at least one auditor/mediator sharing an encryption key with the asset). When true, $r_2/r_1$ is committed. It can be true even without visibility because auditor ephemeral keys still need the $r_2$ component proven relative to $r_1$ — the proof $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1] = \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \cdot (r_2/r_1)$ requires the ratio as a witness.
  - `commit_r_1_r_2_inv` = `receiver_sees_sender`. When true, $r_1/r_2$ is committed. Auditor keys never need this ratio, so it is gated purely on receiver-sees-sender.

1. Creator picks 3 (or 4) independent random scalars $r_1, r_2, r_3 \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ (and optionally $r_4 \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ if asset-id is encrypted) and creates ciphertexts:
   $$\mathrm{CT}_s = r_1 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{EK}_s$$
   $$\mathrm{CT}_r = r_2 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{EK}_r$$
   $$\mathrm{CT}_v = r_3 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + v \cdot H$$
   $$\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}} = r_4 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{at} \cdot H \quad \text{(only if asset-id is not revealed)}$$

   These follow the twisted Elgamal pattern with the same ephemeral randomness $r_i$ shared across all parties. 

2. For each asset encryption key $\mathrm{EK}_i$ (auditors, and mediators sharing a key), create ephemeral keys to allow them to decrypt $\mathrm{CT}_s, \mathrm{CT}_r, \mathrm{CT}_v$ (and optionally $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$ if asset-id is encrypted):
   $$\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i} = (\mathrm{EK}_i \cdot r_1, \mathrm{EK}_i \cdot r_2, \mathrm{EK}_i \cdot r_3, \mathrm{EK}_i \cdot r_4)$$
   Each auditor/mediator who holds $\mathrm{ek}_i$ can compute $\mathrm{ek}_i^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[j] = r_j \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$ and subtract it from the corresponding $\mathrm{CT}$ to decrypt.

3. Similarly, sender and receiver each get their own ephemeral keys:
   - Sender gets: $\mathrm{Eph}_s = (\mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_1, \mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_3, \mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_4)$ ($\mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_2$ is included when `sender_sees_receiver`)
   - Receiver gets: $\mathrm{Eph}_r = (\mathrm{EK}_r \cdot r_2, \mathrm{EK}_r \cdot r_3, \mathrm{EK}_r \cdot r_4)$ ($\mathrm{EK}_r \cdot r_1$ is included when `receiver_sees_sender`)

4. For each mediator affirmation key $\mathrm{MK}_j$, creator picks a fresh independent random $r_{m_j} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates a single ciphertext of the affirmation key per asset encryption key $\mathrm{EK}_i$ (same idea as twisted Elgamal):
   $$\mathrm{CT}_{m_j} = r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{MK}_j$$
   $$\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j} = (\mathrm{EK}_1 \cdot r_{m_j}, \mathrm{EK}_2 \cdot r_{m_j}, \dots , \mathrm{EK}_n \cdot r_{m_j})$$
   The mediator holds $\mathrm{ek}_k$ for exactly one index $k$; using the $k$-th ephemeral key it recovers $r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_k^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k]$ and learns $\mathrm{MK}_j = \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} - r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$. Because an ephemeral key is provided for every $\mathrm{EK}_i$, the leg does not reveal which index the mediator holds.

5. For public encryption keys (auditors who don't need encrypted randomness), create ephemeral keys directly:
   $$\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i} = (\mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_1, \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_2, \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_3, \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_4)$$
   where $\mathrm{PK}_i$ is the i-th public encryption key (the $r_4$ component is omitted when asset-id is revealed). The public auditor recovers $r_j \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = (\mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_j) \cdot \mathrm{ek}_i^{-1}$ using its secret key.

The full leg encryption is:
$$
(\mathrm{CT}_s, \mathrm{CT}_r, \mathrm{CT}_v, \mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}, \mathrm{Eph}_s, \mathrm{Eph}_r, [\mathrm{Eph}_{e_0}, \mathrm{Eph}_{e_1}, \dots ], [\mathrm{CT}_{m_0}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_0}, \mathrm{CT}_{m_1}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_1}, \dots ],$$
$$[\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_0}, \mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_1}, \dots ])
$$
where each $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j} = (\mathrm{EK}_1 \cdot r_{m_j}, \dots , \mathrm{EK}_n \cdot r_{m_j})$ is itself a vector of $n$ ephemeral keys (one per asset encryption key).

### Decryption
Sender/receiver knows their encryption key pair $(\mathrm{ek}, \mathrm{EK})$. The leg encryption itself doesn't tell a recipient whether they are the sender or the receiver — unless the leg creator informs them out of band, they have to try both roles and the one that recovers their own $\mathrm{EK}$ is the correct one.

**As sender** (try first):
1. Recover $r_1 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_s^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_s[0]$ (since $\mathrm{ek}_s^{-1} \cdot (\mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_1) = r_1 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$), then check $\mathrm{CT}_s - r_1 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{EK}_s$. If equality holds, the recipient is the sender of this leg; otherwise they are not.
2. Recover $r_3 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_s^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_s[2]$ and $r_4 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_s^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_s[3]$ (when asset-id is hidden), then decrypt amount and asset-id by solving discrete log in:
   $$v \cdot H = \mathrm{CT}_v - r_3 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$$
   $$\mathrm{at} \cdot H = \mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}} - r_4 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$$
   Since $v$ and $\mathrm{at}$ are small (48-bit and 32-bit respectively), discrete log is feasible in reasonable time.

**As receiver** (if sender check failed): repeat the same steps with $\mathrm{Eph}_r[1]$ instead of $\mathrm{Eph}_s[0]$ to recover $r_2 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$ and check $\mathrm{CT}_r - r_2 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{EK}_r$; if yes the recipient is the receiver. Amount/asset-id decryption then uses $\mathrm{Eph}_r[2]$ and $\mathrm{Eph}_r[3]$ in place of $\mathrm{Eph}_s[2]$, $\mathrm{Eph}_s[3]$.

For auditors/mediators with encryption key pair $(\mathrm{ek}_i, \mathrm{EK}_i)$:

1. Decrypt $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$ first (when asset-id is hidden) to check whether this leg is for an asset they care about — i.e. whether the recovered $\mathrm{at}$ matches an asset for which they are an auditor/mediator. This acts as a filter: if the asset doesn't match, they skip the rest. 
2. If the asset matches, decrypt $\mathrm{CT}_s, \mathrm{CT}_r, \mathrm{CT}_v$ in the same way: compute $r_j \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_i^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[j]$ for $j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ and subtract from the corresponding ciphertext.
3. If mediator, additionally recover the affirmation key using the ephemeral key at its own encryption-key index $k$: $r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} = \mathrm{ek}_k^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k]$, then $\mathrm{MK}_j = \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} - r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$. The mediator can find its index by trying each $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k]$ until the recovered $\mathrm{MK}_j$ matches its own affirmation key.

## Leg creation proof
The proof for leg creation needs to hide:

- The sender and receiver's public key
- The amount being transferred
- The asset being transacted. This is the most complicated part as it needs to prove that all the transaction details are being 
  encrypted for the asset's auditors and mediators without revealing the asset or identity of auditors and mediators. This can be made 
  optional by leg creator however for efficiency.

Key complexity when asset-id is hidden: Here the asset's auditor encryption keys $\mathrm{EK}_i$ and mediator affirmation keys $\mathrm{MK}_j$ must also be kept hidden from the verifier — revealing them would identify the asset. The proof therefore works over re-randomized versions $E_i$ and $M_j$ (derived from the asset leaf via the Pedersen-commitment-to-curve-points machinery described below) and proves each leg ephemeral key was built from the corresponding hidden key without exposing it. This is the source of most of the steps 5–11: each asset leaf point is re-randomized as $E_i = \mathrm{EK}_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$ and $M_j = \mathrm{MK}_j + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$. When asset-id is revealed the asset's keys $\mathrm{EK}_i, \mathrm{MK}_j$ are public inputs. The asset's public encryption keys ($\mathrm{public\_enc\_keys}$) are always revealed regardless, since they were specified directly by the leg creator and are not part of the asset leaf.

Recall from the "System model" section that an asset's data is stored in a leaf of the asset curve tree. The leaf commits, under a single Pedersen commitment, both coordinates of a list of points (the asset-id point, the auditor encryption keys and the mediator affirmation keys — the "point block"), followed by the mediator count $m$ committed as a scalar under one trailing generator:
$$
\mathrm{Leaf}_{\mathrm{asset\_id}} = \underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{n+m} \left( (P_i.x) \cdot \widetilde{G}_{2i} + (P_i.y) \cdot \widetilde{G}_{2i+1} \right)}_{\text{point block, 2 generators per point}} + \underbrace{m \cdot \widetilde{G}_{2(1+n+m)}}_{\text{mediator count}}, \quad \mathrm{Leaf}_{\mathrm{asset\_id}} \in \mathbb{G}_q
$$
where the point-block points are constructed as:
- $P_0 = (\mathrm{asset\_id} + 1) \cdot J_0$ (the single generator $J_0$ lifts the scalar into $\mathbb{G}_p$; the $+1$ offset keeps $P_0$ off the identity even when $\mathrm{asset\_id} = 0$, which the both-coordinates leaf commitment rejects — see below. The leg proof subtracts $J_0$ so the bound asset-id is the un-offset $\mathrm{asset\_id}$.)
- For each auditor $i$: $P_i = \mathrm{EK}_i$
- For each mediator $j$: $P_{n+j} = \mathrm{MK}_j$ (the bare affirmation public key, committed exactly like an auditor key)

Both coordinates of each point are committed (2 leaf generators per point), which pins the point fully. The mediator's encryption-key index is **not** committed anywhere in the leaf — the broadcast mediator ciphertext (Encryption step 4) removes the need to store it. Only the mediator count $m$ is committed, under the single trailing generator $\widetilde{G}_{2(1+n+m)}$, so the verifier can bind how many mediator entries the leg must carry.

Now this pushes $\mathrm{Leaf}_{\mathrm{asset\_id}}$ in a different group $\mathbb{G}_q$ (Vesta curve) whereas all public keys are in $\mathbb{G}_p$ (Pallas curve). We use a similar technique to the Curve tree paper. The key idea is that the asset-id and encryption keys for all auditors and mediators are committed in a single Pedersen commitment where the **coordinates** of an item "represent" the item, and then we randomize each item and can treat it as a group element which can be revealed. Since the $\mathrm{asset\_id} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, we shift it to $\mathbb{G}_p$ by multiplying it by a public $J_0 \in \mathbb{G}_p$. Following describes the general protocol

**Pedersen Commitment to Curve Points**

Commits to elliptic curve points by committing to **both** their coordinates. Then the knowledge of those committed points can be proven along with generating a re-randomized version of each point.
Given points $P_i \in \mathbb{G}_p$ as 

$$A = [P_0, P_1, \dots , P_n]$$ 

Commit to $A$ in a Pedersen commitment of both coordinates of each $P_i$ (two generators per point), i.e. 

$$C = \mathrm{PedCom}(P_0.x, P_0.y, \dots , P_n.x, P_n.y) = \sum_i \left( \widetilde{G}_{2i} \cdot P_i.x + \widetilde{G}_{2i+1} \cdot P_i.y \right)$$ 

where $C \in \mathbb{G}_q$ and $P_i.x, P_i.y \in \mathbb{Z}_q$ where $\mathbb{G}_p, \mathbb{G}_q$ are on 2 curves which form a 2-cycle (base field of one equals scalar field of other).

1. Prover randomizes each $P_i$ to get 
  $$A_r = [{P_r}_0, {P_r}_1, \dots , {P_r}_n]$$
  
  where 
  
  $$A_r[i] = P_{r_i} = P_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$$ 

  $$B_{\mathrm{blinding}} \in \mathbb{G}_p, \quad \mathrm{bl}_i \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} \mathbb{Z}_p$$.

2. Prover proves $\forall i, P_i \in \mathbb{G}_p$, i.e. $P_i.x, P_i.y$ are x and y coordinates of a point which lies in group $\mathbb{G}_p$ and $P_i.x, P_i.y \in \mathbb{Z}_q$.
3. Prover proves $\forall i, A_r[i] = A[i] + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} = P_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$
4. For a chosen subset $S$ of the indices, the prover additionally outputs, for each $i \in S$, the same blinding $\mathrm{bl}_i$ committed under a **different** generator $B \in \mathbb{G}_p$ (with $B \neq B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$):
  $$\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$$
  and proves (a shared-scalar discrete-log relation in the same circuit) that this $\mathrm{bl}_i$ is the *same* scalar used in $A_r[i] = P_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$. The values $\{\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B\}_{i \in S}$ are public. They let a caller bind some *other* witness to the re-randomization blinding $\mathrm{bl}_i$ — by checking that witness against $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$ — without revealing $\mathrm{bl}_i$. The leg creation proof uses this to pin the Bulletproof blinding witnesses $l_i$ / $k_j$ to the actual re-randomization blindings (see steps 6, 11 below).

Both coordinates are committed, which pins each $P_i$ fully. Account curve trees commit only $(P_i + \Delta).x$ for a public shift $\Delta$, leaving the y-sign free, so a prover could re-randomize $-(P_i + \Delta)$ and pass off a re-randomization of $-P_i - 2\Delta$; that $\Delta$-shift trick does not work here because the prover need not know the opening of the points. Committing both coordinates removes the shift and rejects reflections. $P_i$ being the identity is rejected since $(0, 0)$ is not on the curve — this is why the asset-id point is offset to $(\mathrm{asset\_id} + 1) \cdot J_0$.

In our usage of the above protocol, $A$ corresponds to the points making up the asset leaf:
- $A[0] = (\mathrm{asset\_id} + 1) \cdot J_0$
- $A[i] = \mathrm{EK}_i$ for each of the $n$ auditor encryption keys
- $A[n+j] = \mathrm{MK}_j$ for each of the $m$ mediators (the bare affirmation key; the mediator's encryption-key index is not committed in the leaf, only the count $m$ is)

The subset $S = \{1, \dots, n+m\}$ — i.e. every auditor and mediator point also gets the second commitment $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$. The asset-id point at index $0$ is excluded since its blinding $\mathrm{bl}_0$ is proven directly in $T_{\mathrm{AT}_r}$ (step 8).

**Notation.** The blindings used to re-randomize the asset leaf points are written $\mathrm{bl}_0, \mathrm{bl}_1, \dots, \mathrm{bl}_{n+m}$ throughout (so $\mathrm{bl}_0$ is the asset-id blinding, $\mathrm{bl}_1, \dots, \mathrm{bl}_n$ are the encryption-key blindings, and $\mathrm{bl}_{n+1}, \dots, \mathrm{bl}_{n+m}$ are the mediator-key blindings, matching $A$'s indexing above). In the Bulletproof commitment (step 9 below) the encryption-key blindings appear as the witnesses $l_i := \mathrm{bl}_{i+1}$ for $i = 0, \dots, n-1$ (the offset is because $\mathrm{bl}_0$ is for the asset-id and is handled separately in $T_{\mathrm{AT}_r}$), and the mediator-key blindings appear as $k_j := \mathrm{bl}_{n+j+1}$ for $j = 0, \dots, m-1$ (not committed in the BP — they are only used in $T_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}$). The mediator encryption randomnesses from the leg encryption are written $m_j := r_{m_j}$ when they appear as BP witnesses. For any witness $x$ used in a Schnorr T-value, we write its random blinding as $x_r$ (e.g. $r_{1_r}$ is the Schnorr blinding for $r_1$); when $x$ itself is a compound expression we write the blinding as $(x)_r$ for clarity (e.g. $(\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot r_1)_r$). The two cross-ratios are committed independently: $r_2/r_1$ iff `commit_r_2_r_1_inv`, $r_1/r_2$ iff `commit_r_1_r_2_inv`. Writing $n_\times \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ for the number of committed cross-ratios, the encryption-key and mediator witnesses in $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ begin at index $\omega := 12 + n_\times$ (so $\omega = 14$ only when both ratios are committed). $r_2/r_1$, when present, is always at index $[12]$; $r_1/r_2$ is at $[13]$ when $r_2/r_1$ is also committed, else at $[12]$.

A prover (settlement creator) wants to prove following relations when creating a leg with amount $v$:

1. $\mathrm{CT}_v, \mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$ correctly encrypt the amount and asset-id (and similarly $\mathrm{CT}_s, \mathrm{CT}_r$ encrypt $\mathrm{EK}_s, \mathrm{EK}_r$).
2. For each auditor with index $i$, each component of $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}$ matches the corresponding leg randomness paired with the i-th auditor's encryption key in the asset leaf: $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1] = r_2 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2] = r_3 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3] = r_4 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$ (the last component is present only when asset-id is hidden). For each mediator $j$, every component of the broadcast ephemeral vector matches the corresponding auditor key: $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l] = r_{m_j} \cdot \mathrm{EK}_l$ for each of the $n$ auditor encryption keys. The same enforcement applies to $\mathrm{Eph}_s, \mathrm{Eph}_r$ against $\mathrm{EK}_s, \mathrm{EK}_r$. This also involves a curve tree membership proof so the verifier knows $\mathrm{EK}_i, \mathrm{MK}_j$ come from a legitimate asset leaf without revealing which asset.
3. The Bulletproof blinding witness $l_i$ used in the $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$ relation equals the actual re-randomization blinding $\mathrm{bl}_i$ of $E_i$, and the mediator witness $k_j$ used in the $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ relation equals $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ of $M_j$. This is the binding that makes relation 2 sound when $\mathrm{EK}_i$/$\mathrm{MK}_j$ are hidden: $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot (E_i - l_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$, and without forcing $l_i = \mathrm{bl}_i$ a malicious creator could pick $l_i \neq \mathrm{bl}_i$ to get $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i + r_1 \cdot (\mathrm{bl}_i - l_i) \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} \neq r_1 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$ — the proof would still verify but auditor $i$ (resp. mediator $j$) would be unable to decrypt. The binding is proven against the public $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$ / $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B$ from the Pedersen-commitment-to-curve-points protocol above.
4. $v \leq \mathrm{MAX\_BALANCE}$


**Instance**: $\mathrm{CT}_s$, $\mathrm{CT}_r$, $\mathrm{CT}_v$, $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$, $\mathrm{Eph}_s$, $\mathrm{Eph}_r$, $[\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}]$, $[\mathrm{CT}_{m_i}]$, $[\mathrm{Eph}_{m_i}]$, $[\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}]$, $A_r = [\mathrm{AT}_r, E_1, \dots , E_n, M_1, \dots , M_m]$ (re-randomized points), $[\mathrm{bl}_1 \cdot B, \dots , \mathrm{bl}_{n+m} \cdot B]$ (blindings under the second generator), $\mathrm{Path}_r$, $\mathrm{Root}$, $G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$, $H$, $J_0$, $B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$, $B$, $m$ (mediator count, committed in the leaf under $\widetilde{G}_{2(1+n+m)}$), curve tree generators

**Witness**: $v, \mathrm{at}, r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4, r_1^{-1}, r_2^{-1}, \mathrm{EK}_s, \mathrm{EK}_r, \mathrm{EK}_i, \mathrm{MK}_j, [r_{m_j}], [\mathrm{bl}_i], \mathrm{Path}$.

Above are the high level instance and witness values known before the protocol starts and don't include instance and witness created during protocol execution like the various blindings and $T$ values.


### Prover
1. Prover creates list of points for asset-id and keys as $$A = [(\mathrm{at}+1) \cdot J_0, \mathrm{EK}_1, \mathrm{EK}_2, \dots \mathrm{EK}_n, \mathrm{MK}_1, \dots , \mathrm{MK}_m]$$
   where $\mathrm{EK}_i$ are auditor encryption keys and $\mathrm{MK}_j$ are mediator affirmation keys. Both coordinates of every point are committed in the leaf, and the mediator count $m$ is committed under the trailing generator; no mediator index is committed.
2. Create blindings $\mathrm{blindings} = [\mathrm{bl}_0, \mathrm{bl}_1, \dots , \mathrm{bl}_{n+m}]$ and randomize $A$ as $$A_r = [\mathrm{AT}_r, E_1, \dots , E_n, M_1, \dots , M_m]$$
   where $\mathrm{AT}_r = (\mathrm{at}+1) \cdot J_0 + \mathrm{bl}_0 \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$, $E_i = \mathrm{EK}_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$, and $M_j = \mathrm{MK}_j + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$. $A_r$ is shared with the verifier.
3. Take both coordinates of all points in
   - $A_r$ as $$x_r=[\mathrm{AT}_r.x, E_1.x, \dots , E_n.x, M_1.x, \dots , M_m.x]$$ and similar for $y_r$
   - $A$ as $$x=[((\mathrm{at}+1) \cdot J_0).x, \mathrm{EK}_1.x, \dots , \mathrm{EK}_n.x, \mathrm{MK}_1.x, \dots , \mathrm{MK}_m.x]$$ and similar for $y$
4. Enforces constraints that for each $i$:
   - $(x_i, y_i)$ is a valid curve point
   - $(x_i, y_i) + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} = (x_{r_i}, y_{r_i})$. The RHS is public but the LHS only has public the fixed point ($B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$) .
5. Since each re-randomized enc-key is $E_i = \mathrm{EK}_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$, the prover can substitute $\mathrm{EK}_i = E_i - \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$. For each of the $n$ auditor encryption keys, the ephemeral key relation is $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i = r_1 \cdot (E_i - \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$.
6. Write $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot E_i + (r_1 \cdot \mathrm{bl}_i) \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$. This is a 2-witness Pedersen commitment with witnesses $(r_1, r_1 \cdot \mathrm{bl}_i)$ and bases $(E_i, -B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$. The product $r_1 \cdot \mathrm{bl}_i$ is enforced by a Bulletproof multiplication constraint. The remaining ephemeral key entries use ratios: $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1] = (r_2/r_1) \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2] = (r_3/r_1) \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3] = (r_4/r_1) \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$.
7. For proving $\mathrm{CT}_s = r_1 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + r_1^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_s[0]$ (where $\mathrm{Eph}_s[0] = \mathrm{EK}_s \cdot r_1$), prover uses witnesses $(r_1, r_1^{-1})$ with bases $(G_{\mathrm{Enc}}, \mathrm{Eph}_s[0])$. Similarly for $\mathrm{CT}_r = r_2 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + r_2^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_r[1]$ using $(r_2, r_2^{-1})$. The constraints $r_1 \cdot r_1^{-1} = 1$ and $r_2 \cdot r_2^{-1} = 1$ are enforced in Bulletproofs.
8. For proving $\mathrm{AT}_r - J_0 = \mathrm{at} \cdot J_0 + \mathrm{bl}_0 \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$ (the $J_0$ is subtracted because the asset-id point is $(\mathrm{at}+1) \cdot J_0$, so this relation binds the un-offset $\mathrm{at}$, shared with the $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$ relation), prover picks random $\mathrm{at}_r, \mathrm{bl}_{0_r} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates:
   $$T_{\mathrm{AT}_r} = \mathrm{at}_r \cdot J_0 + \mathrm{bl}_{0_r} \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}, \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
9. For the unified Bulletproof commitment, prover commits to the witness array:
   $$\mathrm{witnesses} = [v, r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4, r_1^{-1}, r_2^{-1}, r_3/r_1, r_3/r_2, r_4/r_1, r_4/r_2, (r_2/r_1)^*, (r_1/r_2)^*, l_0, l_0 \cdot r_1, \dots , l_{n-1}, l_{n-1} \cdot r_1, m_0, m_0 / r_1, \dots , m_{m-1}, m_{m-1} / r_1]$$
   where $(r_2/r_1)^*$ is present iff `commit_r_2_r_1_inv` and $(r_1/r_2)^*$ iff `commit_r_1_r_2_inv` (independently), so the trailing $l_\bullet, m_\bullet$ witnesses begin at index $\omega = 12 + n_\times$ (see Notation). The commitment to $\mathrm{witnesses}$ is:
   $$C_{\mathrm{BP}} = b \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} + v \cdot G_1 + r_1 \cdot G_2 + r_2 \cdot G_3 + \dots \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
   where $b$ is a fresh random blinding and $G_i$ are Bulletproof generators.
10. Bulletproof constraints enforce:
    - $r_1 \cdot r_1^{-1} = 1$ and $r_2 \cdot r_2^{-1} = 1$
    - $r_1 \cdot (r_3/r_1) = r_3$ and $r_2 \cdot (r_3/r_2) = r_3$
    - $r_1 \cdot (r_4/r_1) = r_4$ and $r_2 \cdot (r_4/r_2) = r_4$
    - If `commit_r_2_r_1_inv`: $r_1 \cdot (r_2/r_1) = r_2$. If `commit_r_1_r_2_inv`: $r_2 \cdot (r_1/r_2) = r_1$
    - For each encryption key: $r_1 \cdot l_i = l_i \cdot r_1$
    - For each mediator: $r_1 \cdot (m_i / r_1) = m_i$
    - $v \leq \mathrm{MAX\_BALANCE}$ (range proof)
11. **Sender/receiver ciphertext proofs**: Prover picks random $r_{1_r}, r_{1^{-1}_r}, r_{2_r}, r_{2^{-1}_r} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_s} = r_{1_r} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + r_{1^{-1}_r} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_s[0] \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
    $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_r} = r_{2_r} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + r_{2^{-1}_r} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_r[1] \in \mathbb{G}_p$$

    **Auditor enc-key ephemeral proofs**: For each of the $n$ auditor enc_keys (with $\mathrm{bl}_i$ denoting the blinding of the $i$-th re-randomized enc-key entry $E_i = \mathrm{EK}_i + \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$ — the same $\mathrm{bl}_i$ appears as the witness $l_i$ in the Bulletproof commitment of step 9), prover proves $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot E_i + (r_1 \cdot \mathrm{bl}_i) \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$. Reusing $r_{1_r}$ from the sender ciphertext proof above (so the $r_1$ response is shared) and picking a fresh blinding $(\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot r_1)_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ for the product witness $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot r_1$, prover creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]} = r_{1_r} \cdot E_i + (\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot r_1)_r \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}}) \in \mathbb{G}_p$$

    **Auditor blinding binding**: The relation above only gives $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] = r_1 \cdot \mathrm{EK}_i$ if the committed $l_i$ equals the true re-randomization blinding $\mathrm{bl}_i$. To enforce that, the prover proves $l_i \cdot B = \mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$ against the public $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$ (whose $\mathrm{bl}_i$ the curve-tree proof already ties to $E_i$). It reuses the Bulletproof commitment's Schnorr blinding $l_{i_r}$ for the $l_i$ witness — so the $l_i$ response is shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ and there is no separate stored response — and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{bl}_i} = l_{i_r} \cdot B \in \mathbb{G}_p$$

    **Mediator ciphertext and ephemeral proofs**: For each of the $m$ mediator entries in the asset leaf (with re-randomized entry $M_j = \mathrm{MK}_j + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$), prover proves:
    - $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ is correctly encrypted: let $D_j = \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} - M_j$ (computable from public values). The prover knows witnesses $(r_{m_j}, \mathrm{bl}_{n+j})$ in $D_j = r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}})$. Prover picks random $r_{m_{j,r}}, \mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates: $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}} = r_{m_{j,r}} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r} \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}}) \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
    - $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ is bound to the leaf re-randomization: the prover proves the $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ witness used in $D_j$ above equals the actual re-randomization blinding of $M_j$, against the public $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B$. Reusing the $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ proof's Schnorr blinding $\mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r}$ (so the response is shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}[1]$), prover creates: $$T_{k_j} = \mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r} \cdot B \in \mathbb{G}_p$$ Without this, a malformed $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ (offset by a multiple of $B_{\mathrm{blinding}}$) would still verify but the mediator would recover a wrong $\mathrm{MK}_j$.
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}$ is correctly formed (broadcast): for each auditor encryption key $l = 1, \dots, n$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l] = r_{m_j} \cdot \mathrm{EK}_l$. Since $\mathrm{EK}_l \cdot r_1 = \mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0]$ (the $r_1$ ephemeral key for auditor $l$, already a public part of the leg encryption), this is equivalent to
      $$\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l] = \mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0] \cdot (r_{m_j}/r_1)$$
      Prover proves all $n$ of these as single-witness discrete logs sharing the one witness $m_j/r_1$ (committed in the Bulletproof commitment, where $m_j = r_{m_j}$), each against its own base $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0]$. Picking a single fresh blinding $(m_j/r_1)_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ reused across all $n$:
      $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}}[l] = (m_j/r_1)_r \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0] \in \mathbb{G}_p \quad \text{for } l = 1, \dots, n$$
      The Bulletproof multiplication constraint $r_1 \cdot (m_j/r_1) = m_j$ binds the $m_j$ used in $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ above to the $m_j/r_1$ used here.

    **Public encryption key proofs**: For each public encryption key $\mathrm{PK}_i$, prover proves the four ephemeral key entries $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[0] = \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_1$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[1] = \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_2$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[2] = \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_3$, $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[3] = \mathrm{PK}_i \cdot r_4$ by reusing $r_{1_r}, r_{2_r}, r_{3_r}, r_{4_r}$ from the sender/receiver/amount/asset-id ciphertext proofs above:
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[0]} = r_{1_r} \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i, \quad T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[1]} = r_{2_r} \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i, \quad T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[2]} = r_{3_r} \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i, \quad T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[3]} = r_{4_r} \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i$$
12. For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]$, prover picks random $(r_2/r_1)_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]} = (r_2/r_1)_r \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
13. For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]$, prover picks random $(r_3/r_1)_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]} = (r_3/r_1)_r \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
14. For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]$, prover picks random $(r_4/r_1)_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]} = (r_4/r_1)_r \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
15. For the Bulletproof commitment $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$, prover picks random blindings for each witness and creates:
     $$T_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}} = b_r \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} + v_r \cdot G_1 + r_{1_r} \cdot G_2 + r_{2_r} \cdot G_3 + r_{3_r} \cdot G_4 + r_{4_r} \cdot G_5 + \dots \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
     where the blindings correspond to the witness array structure from step 9.
16. For proving $\mathrm{CT}_v = r_3 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + v \cdot H$ and $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}} = r_4 \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{at} \cdot H$, prover picks random $$r_{3_r}, v_r, r_{4_r} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$$ and creates:
    $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_v} = r_{3_r} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + v_r \cdot H \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
    $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}} = r_{4_r} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{at}_r \cdot H \in \mathbb{G}_p$$
    Note: $\mathrm{at}_r$ was already created in step 8 for the asset-id commitment $T_{\mathrm{AT}_r}$.
17. Prover hashes the following to create challenge $c$ as:
    $$c = \mathrm{Hash}(A_r, \mathrm{Path}_r, \mathrm{Root}, \mathrm{Leg\_Enc}, C_{\mathrm{BP}}, T_{\mathrm{AT}_r}, T_{\mathrm{CT}_s}, T_{\mathrm{CT}_r},$$
    $$T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]}, T_{\mathrm{bl}_i}, T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]}, T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]}, T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]}, $$
    $$T_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}, T_{k_j}, [T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}}[l]]_{l=1}^{n}, T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[j]},$$
    $$T_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}, T_{\mathrm{CT}_v}, T_{\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}})$$
    where $\mathrm{Leg\_Enc}$ is the full leg encryption structure.
18. Prover creates responses:
    - For $\mathrm{AT}_r$ (full Pedersen commitment proof):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{AT}} = [\mathrm{at}_r + \mathrm{at} \cdot c, \mathrm{bl}_{0_r} + \mathrm{bl}_0 \cdot c]$$
    - For $\mathrm{CT}_s$ (partial proof, witnesses from $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_s} = [\perp, \perp]$$
      (witnesses $r_1, r_1^{-1}$ are at indices [2], [6] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
    - For $\mathrm{CT}_r$ (partial proof, witnesses from $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_r} = [\perp, \perp]$$
      (witnesses $r_2, r_2^{-1}$ are at indices [3], [7] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
    - For $\mathrm{CT}_v$ (partial proof, witnesses from $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_v} = [\perp, \perp]$$
      (witnesses $r_3, v$ are at indices [4], [1] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
    - For $\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}$ (partial proof):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}} = [\perp, \perp]$$
      (witness $r_4$ is at index [5] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$, and $\mathrm{at}$ from $\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{AT}}[0]$)
19. For each enc_key $i$, prover creates responses:
    - For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$ (partial 2-witness Pedersen proof — **both** responses are shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$, so only the commitment $T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]}$ is stored): the $r_1$ response is $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[2]$ and the product $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot r_1$ response is $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2i + 1]$ (the committed $l_i \cdot r_1$ witness):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]} = [\perp, \perp]$$
    - For $T_{\mathrm{bl}_i}$ (binding $l_i = \mathrm{bl}_i$): no separate response is stored — the $l_i$ response is reused from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ (the $l_{i_r} + l_i \cdot c$ entry, at index $[\omega + 2i]$). Only the commitment $T_{\mathrm{bl}_i}$ is part of the proof.
    - For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]$ (partial discrete log proof):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]} = (r_2/r_1)_r + (r_2/r_1) \cdot c$$
      (witness $r_2/r_1$ is at index [12] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$; present because auditor keys force `commit_r_2_r_1_inv`)
    - For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]$ (partial discrete log proof):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]} = (r_3/r_1)_r + (r_3/r_1) \cdot c$$
      (witness $r_3/r_1$ is at index [8] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
    - For $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]$ (partial discrete log proof):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]} = (r_4/r_1)_r + (r_4/r_1) \cdot c$$
      (witness $r_4/r_1$ is at index [10] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)

    For each mediator $j$, prover creates responses:
    - For $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ (Partial2 Pedersen commitment — the $r_{m_j}$ response is shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2n + 2j]$ (the committed $m_j$ witness), so only the $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ response is stored):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}} = [\perp, \mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r} + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot c]$$
    - For $T_{k_j}$ (binding $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$): no separate response is stored — it reuses $\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}[1]$ (the $\mathrm{bl}_{(n+j),r} + \mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot c$ entry). Only the commitment $T_{k_j}$ is part of the proof.
    - For $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l]$ (partial discrete log proofs, one per auditor key $l = 1, \dots, n$): all $n$ reuse a single response
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}} = (m_j / r_1)_r + (m_j / r_1) \cdot c$$
      (witness $m_j / r_1$ is at index $[\omega + 2n + 2j + 1]$ of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$), so only the $n$ commitments $T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}}[l]$ are part of the proof, no separate responses.
20. For public encryption keys:
    - For each public enc key $i$ (no separate responses are stored — all four are reused from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$):
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}} = [\perp, \perp, \perp, \perp]$$
      (witnesses $r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4$ are at $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ indices [2], [3], [4], [5])
21. For relation $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$, prover creates response array:
    $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}} = [b_r + b \cdot c, v_r + v \cdot c, r_{1_r} + r_1 \cdot c, r_{2_r} + r_2 \cdot c, r_{3_r} + r_3 \cdot c, r_{4_r} + r_4 \cdot c,$$
    $$r_{1^{-1}_r} + r_1^{-1} \cdot c, r_{2^{-1}_r} + r_2^{-1} \cdot c, (r_3/r_1)_r + (r_3/r_1) \cdot c, (r_3/r_2)_r + (r_3/r_2) \cdot c,$$
    $$(r_4/r_1)_r + (r_4/r_1) \cdot c, (r_4/r_2)_r + (r_4/r_2) \cdot c, ((r_2/r_1)_r + (r_2/r_1) \cdot c)^*, ((r_1/r_2)_r + (r_1/r_2) \cdot c)^*,$$
    $$l_{0_r} + l_0 \cdot c, (l_0 \cdot r_1)_r + (l_0 \cdot r_1) \cdot c, \dots , m_{0_r} + m_0 \cdot c, (m_0 / r_1)_r + (m_0 / r_1) \cdot c, \dots ]$$
    where $(r_2/r_1)^*$ is present iff `commit_r_2_r_1_inv` and $(r_1/r_2)^*$ iff `commit_r_1_r_2_inv`; the subsequent $l_\bullet, m_\bullet$ entries therefore begin at index $\omega = 12 + n_\times$. Index [0] is the blinding $b$.
22. The proof is:
    $$(\mathrm{r1cs\_proof}, \mathrm{re\_randomized\_path}, $$
    $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_s}, \mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_r}, \mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_v}, \mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{\mathrm{at}}}, \mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{AT}}, \mathrm{re\_randomized\_points}, \mathrm{ped\_comms}, $$
    $$C_{\mathrm{BP}}, T_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}, \mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}, $$

    where $\mathrm{r1cs\_proof}$ contains the Bulletproof R1CS proof, $\mathrm{re\_randomized\_path}$ is the curve tree path proof, $\mathrm{re\_randomized\_points}$ contains the primary ($A_r$) re-randomized points together with the second-generator blindings $\{\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B\}$, and $\mathrm{ped\_comms}$ are the Pedersen commitment proofs for the curve points. The binding commitments $T_{\mathrm{bl}_i}, T_{k_j}$ are stored, but their responses are reused ($l_i$ from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$, $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ from $\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}[1]$). Likewise the $T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[j]}$ values are part of the transcript but their responses are reused from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$, so no separate responses are stored.

   (witness $r_4$ at index [5], $\mathrm{at}$ from $\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{AT}}[0]$)

     $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[2] \cdot E_i + \mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2i + 1] \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}}) \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \cdot c$$
     (both responses shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$: witness $r_1$ at index [2], product $r_1 \cdot \mathrm{bl}_i$ at index $[\omega + 2i + 1]$)
   - Binding $l_i = \mathrm{bl}_i$:
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2i] \cdot B \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{bl}_i} + (\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B) \cdot c$$
     (reuses the $l_i$ response at index $[\omega + 2i]$; $\mathrm{bl}_i \cdot B$ is the public second-generator blinding for leaf index $i+1$. This forces the $l_i$ used in $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0]$ to equal the re-randomization blinding $\mathrm{bl}_i$ of $E_i$.)
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]$:
      $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[1] \cdot c$$
     (witness $r_2/r_1$ at index [12] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
   - $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]$:
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[2] \cdot c$$
     (witness $r_3/r_1$ at index [8] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)
   - $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]$:
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[0] \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{e_i}[3] \cdot c$$
     (witness $r_4/r_1$ at index [10] of $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$)

9. For each mediator $j$, verifies:
   - $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ (Partial2 commitment): Let $D_j = \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} - M_j$, then (the $r_{m_j}$ response is shared from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2n + 2j]$):
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2n + 2j] \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}[1] \cdot (-B_{\mathrm{blinding}}) \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}} + D_j \cdot c$$
   - Binding $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$:
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}}[1] \cdot B \stackrel{?}{=} T_{k_j} + (\mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B) \cdot c$$
     (reuses the $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ response from the $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ proof; $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j} \cdot B$ is the public second-generator blinding for leaf index $n+j+1$. This forces the $\mathrm{bl}_{n+j}$ used in $D_j$ to equal the re-randomization blinding of $M_j$.)
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l]$ (broadcast): for each auditor encryption key $l = 1, \dots, n$, with base $\mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0]$ (auditor $l$'s $r_1$ ephemeral key from the leg encryption), all reusing the single $(m_j/r_1)$ response from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ at index $[\omega + 2n + 2j + 1]$:
     $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[\omega + 2n + 2j + 1] \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{e_l}[0] \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}}[l] + \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[l] \cdot c$$

10. For each public encryption key $i$, verifies (using $r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4$ responses from $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ at indices [2], [3], [4], [5]):
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[0]$: $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[2] \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[0]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[0] \cdot c$$
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[1]$: $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[3] \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[1]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[1] \cdot c$$
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[2]$: $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[4] \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[2]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[2] \cdot c$$
    - $\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[3]$: $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[5] \cdot \mathrm{PK}_i \stackrel{?}{=} T_{\mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[3]} + \mathrm{Eph}_{\mathrm{public\_e}_i}[3] \cdot c$$

11. Verifies the Bulletproof commitment $C_{\mathrm{BP}}$ using the response array $\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}$ and generators:
    $$\mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[0] \cdot B_{\mathrm{blinding}} + \mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[1] \cdot G_1 + \mathrm{resp}_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}}[2] \cdot G_2 + \dots \stackrel{?}{=} T_{C_{\mathrm{BP}}} + C_{\mathrm{BP}} \cdot c$$
    where the generators $G_i$ correspond to the witness array structure from the prover step 9. This verifies the Schnorr protocol for the Bulletproof commitment.

12. Verifier finally checks the Bulletproofs R1CS proof validates all arithmetic constraints (curve point validity, randomization consistency, curve tree membership, multiplication relations, and range proof).


## Mediator affirmation proof

A mediator for an asset can either accept or reject a leg in a settlement. The proof shows that the actor really is a mediator for this leg — i.e. that they hold both:

- $\mathrm{enc\_sk}$ — an encryption secret key whose public key $\mathrm{EK}_k$ is **some** auditor encryption key in the asset leaf, at an index $k$ the mediator keeps private (the leg encryption gives this mediator $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] = \mathrm{EK}_k \cdot r_{m_j}$), and
- $\mathrm{m\_sk}$ — the mediator's affirmation secret key whose public key $\mathrm{MK} = \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$ is the affirmation key encrypted in this leg's $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j} = r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{MK}$.

Because the mediator ciphertext is now broadcast (an ephemeral key $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[i] = \mathrm{EK}_i \cdot r_{m_j}$ for every auditor encryption key), the proof must not reveal which index $k$ the mediator holds — revealing it would leak information about the asset. So the proof is a CDS 1-of-$n$ over the $n$ auditor encryption keys. For the branch at the mediator's own index $k$, observe that the mediator can rewrite $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ using its two secrets:
$$\mathrm{CT}_{m_j} = r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} + \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}} = \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] + \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$$
since $\mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] = \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot (\mathrm{EK}_k \cdot r_{m_j}) = \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot (\mathrm{enc\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}} \cdot r_{m_j}) = r_{m_j} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Enc}}$. The ring proves that there exists some index $k$ for which knowledge of $(\mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1}, \mathrm{m\_sk})$ satisfies
$$\mathrm{CT}_{m_j} = \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] + \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$$
without disclosing $k$. This simultaneously demonstrates the actor holds one of the asset's encryption secret keys and the affirmation secret key (for the $\mathrm{MK}$ encrypted in $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$). The affirmation key $G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$ (the ring's shared second base) and the ciphertext $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ (the ring's shared committed value $y$) are the same across every branch; only the first base $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k]$ differs per branch.

CDS protocol of Theorem 8 and Corollary 12 of [Proofs of Partial Knowledge and Simplified Design of Witness Hiding Protocols](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/voting/papers/CramerDamgardSchoenmakers-ProofsOfPartialKnowledge.pdf) is used.

The ring is a standard CDS 1-of-$n$ Schnorr-style two-witness relations: for the real branch $k$ the prover uses the true witnesses $(\mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1}, \mathrm{m\_sk})$, and for every other branch it simulates by choosing that branch's challenge and responses at random and back-solving its commitment. The overall challenge is split so the per-branch challenges sum to the Fiat–Shamir hash.

**Instance**: $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j} = (\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[1], \dots, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[n]), \mathrm{accept}/\mathrm{reject}, G_{\mathrm{Enc}}, G_{\mathrm{Aff}}, j$ (the mediator's slot in the leg), $\mathrm{nonce}$

**Witness**: $\mathrm{enc\_sk}, k$ (the private index of the encryption key the mediator holds), $\mathrm{m\_sk}$ (the prover supplies the raw $\mathrm{enc\_sk}$; the inversion to $\mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1}$ happens inside the proof, and $k$ is never revealed)

Above are the high level instance and witness values known before the protocol starts and don't include instance and witness created during protocol execution like blindings, per-branch challenges and $T$ values.

### Prover

1. Mediator indicates its acceptance or rejection of the leg. The accept/reject flag, the mediator slot $j$ (as a little-endian `u32`) and $\mathrm{nonce}$ are added to the transcript.
2. For every branch $i \neq k$ (simulated), prover picks random $c_i, s_{1,i}, s_{2,i} \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and back-solves the commitment
   $$t_i = \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[i] \cdot s_{1,i} + G_{\mathrm{Aff}} \cdot s_{2,i} - \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} \cdot c_i$$
   For the real branch $k$, prover picks random $r_1, r_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and sets
   $$t_k = \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] \cdot r_1 + G_{\mathrm{Aff}} \cdot r_2$$
3. Prover hashes the following to create the overall challenge $c$ as:
   $$c = \mathrm{Hash}(\mathrm{accept}/\mathrm{reject}, j, \mathrm{nonce}, G_{\mathrm{Aff}}, \mathrm{CT}_{m_j}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[1], \dots, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[n], t_1, \dots, t_n)$$
   ($\mathrm{nonce}$ is the usual application-level context; hashing the full $(\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j})$ binds the proof to this leg's mediator entry.)
4. Prover fixes the real branch's challenge so all branch challenges sum to $c$, then completes the real branch's responses:
   $$c_k = c - \sum_{i \neq k} c_i, \qquad s_{1,k} = r_1 + c_k \cdot \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1}, \qquad s_{2,k} = r_2 + c_k \cdot \mathrm{m\_sk}$$
5. The proof is the three length-$n$ vectors
   $$(\, [c_1, \dots, c_n],\ [s_{1,1}, \dots, s_{1,n}],\ [s_{2,1}, \dots, s_{2,n}] \,)$$

### Verifier

1. Verifier reads $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ and the broadcast vector $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}$ for the mediator at slot $j$ from the leg encryption, and rejects if $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j}$ or any $\mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[i]$ is the identity (the ring's extractor needs $r_{m_j} \neq 0$).
2. Verifier adds the accept/reject flag, the slot $j$ (as a little-endian `u32`) and $\mathrm{nonce}$ to the transcript.
3. Verifier recomputes each branch commitment from the responses:
   $$t_i = \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[i] \cdot s_{1,i} + G_{\mathrm{Aff}} \cdot s_{2,i} - \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} \cdot c_i \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, n$$
4. Verifier accepts iff the branch challenges sum to the Fiat–Shamir hash:
   $$\sum_{i=1}^{n} c_i \stackrel{?}{=} \mathrm{Hash}(\mathrm{accept}/\mathrm{reject}, j, \mathrm{nonce}, G_{\mathrm{Aff}}, \mathrm{CT}_{m_j}, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[1], \dots, \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[n], t_1, \dots, t_n)$$

For the real branch $k$, $t_k = \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] \cdot s_{1,k} + G_{\mathrm{Aff}} \cdot s_{2,k} - \mathrm{CT}_{m_j} \cdot c_k$ reproduces the prover's commitment exactly because $\mathrm{CT}_{m_j} = \mathrm{enc\_sk}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{Eph}_{m_j}[k] + \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$; the simulated branches reproduce their commitments by construction.

**Note on other variants.** When the asset-id is **revealed**, the mediator's affirmation key $\mathrm{MK}$ is public, so a mediator affirms with a plain Schnorr proof of knowledge of $\mathrm{m\_sk}$ in $\mathrm{MK} = \mathrm{m\_sk} \cdot G_{\mathrm{Aff}}$ (no ring is needed).
